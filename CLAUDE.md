# Open360 — Project Overview & Architecture Guide

## 1. Purpose & Scope

Open360 is an open-source Android application (`com.open360`, Jetpack Compose + Kotlin) designed to replace the official Insta360 GPS Action Remote and commercial watch remote apps (`com.kotlin.insta360gpswatchremote`, `com.aktuna.wear.insta360btctrl`) for Insta360 cameras (GO 3, GO 3S, X3, X4, Ace).

Its primary objective is embedding real-time GPS coordinates directly into recorded video metadata (`.insv`/`.mp4`) while providing camera wake-up, shutter, mode switching, and live recording/battery status tracking over BLE without requiring Wi-Fi live preview.

## 2. Core Protocol Architecture

Insta360 cameras have two completely separate BLE architectures. Open360 uses Architecture B on the GO 3S:

| Mode | Role | GATT Service | GPS Injection Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture A (GPS Remote) | Phone = GATT Server (Peripheral)<br>Camera = Central Client | `0xCE80`, `0xD0FF` | What the real remote does: the camera subscribes to `0xCE82` and stores the streamed NMEA. Not usable from a phone on the GO 3S: the camera never opened a link to the phone. The server code is kept but not started. |
| Architecture B (Direct Control) | Phone = Central Client<br>Camera = Peripheral Server | `0xBE80` (`0xBE81`/`0xBE82`) | Works. `UploadGps` (command `0x35`) over `0xBE81` is stored in the clip. This is how the official app does it. |

### Status on the GO 3S (Verified 2026-10-12, Firmware v9.0.59)

- **Architecture B stores GPS.** Three clips recorded while Open360 was connected each contain GPS records (trailer record `0x700`) matching what the app sent. Clips recorded without it contain none.
- **`UploadGps` format.** Protobuf with one field, `bytes gps = 1`, holding 53-byte little-endian records: unix seconds (8), milliseconds (2), status `A`/`V` (1), latitude (8), `N`/`S` (1), longitude (8), `E`/`W` (1), speed m/s (8), track degrees (8), altitude m (8). An earlier attempt that sent three raw doubles as the payload stored nothing.
- **How the camera and Studio treat the stream.** The camera keeps every GPS record it receives from the start command to the stop command, in arrival order (a clip held exactly the 435 records sent in that window). The first video frame comes about 1.8 s after the start command (GPS kept minus video length was 1.75 to 1.81 s on five clips). Insta360 Studio lines the records up with the video from the clip's `first_gps_timestamp` (metadata `0x101` field 36, Unix milliseconds), which it takes as the time of the first frame: at each moment it shows the record whose time is that plus the video time, and its Date & Time overlay is that plus the video time. On every clip up to 2026-10-12 the camera set the field to the first kept record's time, so this looked like counting 100 ms per record from the first; since 2026-10-14 it has not (see Studio and `first_gps_timestamp` below). Arrival times after the first do not matter, but the pace does: the camera drops records that arrive in a burst. After 20 records in 2 s it took 20 more in half a second and then lost the next 33, which shifted the rest of that clip's GPS by 3.3 s.
- **What the app sends.** Nothing while idle. For a recording, record k is the position at `stream start + k * 100 ms`, interpolated between the real fix before and after that moment (`GpsTimeline`, `GpsStreamSchedule`). It holds the position from 0.55 s after that moment (`GPS_BEHIND_PICTURE_MS`, from the ride check below). Since 2026-10-17 the stream starts before the first frame and each record is stamped with its moment moved onto the camera's clock (Placing the GPS by the camera's clock, below); before that it started at the estimated first frame and carried the time of the position it held. Records become ready each time a new fix arrives and are sent at no more than 12 a second, at most four together (`GpsSendPacer`), so the backlog after a GPS outage does not arrive as a burst. The pacing has not yet been tested on the camera after an outage. A moment with no fix within 2.5 s on both sides is sent void, after waiting at most 4 s. Nothing is predicted, so the moments after the last fix that arrived before the stop, about 1 to 2 s, have no records.
- **Finding the first frame (until 2026-10-17; the app no longer places the GPS this way).** Counted from the camera's start notice (notification `0x2026`, field 1 = 2), and it depends on who started the recording. Started from the app, the notice comes about 0.2 s after the start command and the first frame 1.64 s after the notice (1.61 and 1.67 s on two clips). Started with the camera's button, the notice comes well into the start-up and the first frame is taken as 0.55 s after it (0.2 to 0.4 s on four clips, raised by 0.2 s after the ride check below); using the app figure there put the GPS about 1.3 s ahead of the picture. Both figures are the stop minus the video length, so they take the video to end at the stop. That has not been checked against a filmed clock. Measured the same way on 2026-10-17 the gaps were 1.92 to 2.02 s in Video and 0.78 to 0.82 s in PRO Video, for app and button starts alike (The camera's clock, below). The recording ends at field 1 = 5 or 6, or capture state 0, except in Interval Photo and Starlapse, which send 5 and 6 after every photo and end at capture state 0.
- **Checked against the camera's motion sensors (ride, 2026-10-12).** A clip holds the camera's own motion, timed against the frames by `first_frame_timestamp`: in Video mode record `0x1900`, one orientation per frame (40 bytes: `u64` microseconds, then a quaternion as 4 doubles); in PRO Video mode record `0x300`, raw accelerometer and gyroscope at 1 kHz (20 bytes: `u64` microseconds, then six `u16` with 32768 as zero, accelerometer first). Lining up its turns and speed changes with the GPS as Studio places it showed the GPS 0.55 s behind the picture on a clip started from the app and about 0.8 s behind on one started with the button. Each video moment is now given the GPS from 0.55 s later (`GPS_BEHIND_PICTURE_MS`) and the button figure was raised by 0.2 s. This rests on two clips and is good to about 0.2 s. Measured again after the change on one clip started with the button (PRO Video, 172 s, 1707 records with none lost): the GPS was 0.04 s behind the picture on turns. A clip started from the app has not been measured since the change. Whether the phone's GPS reports late or the video starts later than worked out is not known. The phone's reported speed agrees with the speed worked out from its own positions to within 0.1 s. Neither clip lost a record: 2097 records for 210.2 s and 1066 for 106.9 s.
- **Capture mode.** Recordings started by the app's start command were all saved as Video (`VID_`, capture state 1), and those started with the camera's button as PRO Video (`PRO_VID_`, capture state 17), with the camera left in PRO Video mode throughout. That was because the app sent its start command empty, which puts a GO 3S back in normal Video; the command carries the mode (see the modes below).
- **No screen or power-off command works on the GO 3S over Bluetooth (checked 2026-10-16, camera on in its Action Pod).** The Remote tab had Screen, Power Off and Next Mode buttons that wrote the GPS Remote's 9-byte button frames (`fc ef fe 86 ...`, section 3) to `0xBE81`. Each was sent once: the camera did not answer, nothing changed on the camera or the pod, and the link held (the link failure 10 s after a screen frame on 2026-10-13 did not happen again). The buttons have been removed. Tried in their place with the debug intent: `CLOSE_OLED` (50) and `OPEN_OLED` (49) were answered 200 and left the pod's screen as it was, on and dark respectively. `SET_STANDBY_MODE` (56, low energy), `SET_SHUTDOWN` (219, only in a newer published proto, `iliakonnov/insta360-nas`) and `GET_SYNC_CAPTURE_MODE` (55) were answered 400, "unknown msg code.". The Insta360 app has no power-off or screen control for the GO 3S. The camera turns the pod's screen off by itself while live preview runs. Switching between Video and Photo does work, with the command below.
- **Switching between Video and Photo (checked 2026-10-16, camera on in its Action Pod).** One `SET_OPTIONS` (command 7) naming a single option. `08 28 12 00` sets `PHOTO_SUB_MODE` (option 40) to 0, single photo, and puts the camera in Photo; `08 29 12 00` sets `VIDEO_SUB_MODE` (option 41) to 0, normal video, and puts it in Video. A sub-mode other than 0 goes in the value, for example `08 29 12 03 c8 02 05` for video sub-mode 5. Sent over Bluetooth from Open360 with the debug intent, twice each way: the camera answered 200 with the option it took (`08 28` or `08 29`), sent `0x2053` with the new mode about 0.9 s after the command, and the owner saw the pod change both times. This is what the Insta360 app sends, captured on Wi-Fi port 6666 while it switched modes in live preview: each of 18 changes came 10 to 40 ms after one of these. Alongside it the app sends `SET_OPTIONS` for options 133 and 134 (`DARK_EIS_ENABLE_GLOBAL`, `METERING_ENABLE_GLOBAL`, both off) and three `SET_PHOTOGRAPHY_OPTIONS` (function mode 1, then 6 for Photo or 7 for Video), but it also sends those when the mode does not change, and the switch from Open360 worked without them. An earlier Bluetooth try looked ignored because it set both options in one message (photo 0, then video 100): the camera took both (`08 28 08 29`), reported Video, and so stayed in Video. `MAIN_MODE` (option 107: 0 Video, 1 Photo) set to Photo is answered 200 with `08 6b` and changes nothing. In the app the Video and Photo choice on the Camera Control card is the switch (`CameraViewModel.setMode`, `GpsRemoteBleManager.setCameraMode`, `buildSetSubModePayload`): with the camera on, choosing one also sends it, back into the sub-mode the camera was last seen in for that mode, so PRO Video stays PRO Video. Nothing is sent to a camera that is off, and the choice is held while a recording runs. The choice follows the camera's own reports, the sub-modes in the status request on connecting and every `0x2053`, so a mode set on the pod shows in the app. It also decides which capture commands the shutter sends (see the modes below). Checked on the phone (2026-10-16, owner watching the pod): connecting with the camera in Photo moved the choice to Photo; Video and Photo switched the pod each way; switching the pod to PRO Video with its own buttons moved the choice to Video (100, 5); and Photo then Video went back to PRO Video, sending video sub-mode 5, twice. The camera reported the new mode 0.1 to 0.25 s after the command that afternoon. Not checked: the choice being held during a recording.
- **The GO 3S's modes and capturing in each (checked 2026-10-16, camera on in its Action Pod).** The pod offers ten. Here they are in the order the Insta360 app lists them, each with the sub-mode that selects it and the command that captures in it (`CameraMode`): Video (video 0; `StartCapture` and `StopCapture` empty, capture state 1, `VID_`); FreeFrame Video (PRO) (video 5; `StartCapture` `08 05` and `StopCapture` `10 05`, state 17, `PRO_VID_`); TimeShift (video 4; `08 04` and `10 04`, state 13, `PRO_VID_`); Timelapse (video 2; `START_TIMELAPSE` 22 and `STOP_TIMELAPSE` 23 with `08 01`, `MOBILE_TIMELAPSE_VIDEO`, state 2, `PRO_VID_`; `STATIC_TIMELAPSE_VIDEO` was answered 500); Slow Motion (video 1; `08 02` and `10 02`, `Capture_MODE_BULLETTIME`, state 7, `PRO_VID_`); Loop Recording (video 6; `08 06` and `10 06`, state 18, `VID_`); Photo (photo 0; `TAKE_PICTURE` empty, state 4); HDR Photo (photo 1; `TAKE_PICTURE` `08 01`, `AEB`, state 5, a bracketed set of `IMG_` files); Interval Photo (photo 2; 22 and 23 with `08 02`, state 3, a photo every few seconds until stopped); and Starlapse (photo 7; 22 and 23 with `08 05`, state 19). The mode field matters: the plain start and take-picture commands put the camera back in Video or Photo within 0.15 s and capture there. In the app a row of chips under Video and Photo shows the chosen group's modes once the camera has reported its own, with that one highlighted; tapping one switches the camera, and the shutter sends that mode's command, labelled Take HDR Photo, Start Interval Photo and so on. A stop uses the stop command of the mode the camera is in, so a run started on the camera can be stopped from the app. Interval Photo and Starlapse count as recordings (capture states 3 and 19 are not photo states), so the shutter reads Stop while they run and GPS is sent throughout (see GPS in these modes, below). The stop reply names the run's first photo (checked 2026-10-17 on two runs), which the GPX is then named after. How it was checked: the owner read each mode's name off the pod while Open360 set the sub-modes one by one; each command above was then sent with the debug intent and judged by the capture state and the saved file; and finally the app's own chips and shutter were driven over adb through all ten, giving the same states and files (clips 121 to 141 that day), judged by the camera's reports rather than on the pod. Twice in one run of switches video sub-mode 4 fell back to 2 by itself 3.5 to 5 s after being set; it held in every later try, and why is not known. Not checked: stopping from the app a run started with the camera's button.
- **GPS in Timelapse, TimeShift, Slow Motion and the photo runs (checked 2026-10-17, clips 119 to 121 and photos 122 to 146, camera standing still, recorded with the app's chips and shutter).** In each the camera keeps every GPS record the app sends from the start command to the stop, as in Video: 1216 records for 121.5 s of Timelapse, 684 for 68.3 s of TimeShift, 219 for 21.8 s of Slow Motion. What Studio does with them was found by rewriting the records in copies of the clips so that record k reads k km/h and 10 + k m (positions moving north to match), then reading its dashboard.
  - Slow Motion is a 200 fps file whose media time is real time (clip 121: 4551 frames, 22.755 s, timescale 200000; metadata frame rate 200, `frame_rate_av_rational` 200000/1000, file group type 17). Studio plays it eight times slower (3:02.008 on its timeline, at 25 fps) but places the GPS by media time: at 0:48.087 on the timeline (6.011 s of media) it showed 66 km/h and 77 m, the record whose time is `first_gps_timestamp` plus 6.01 s, and at 2:23.101 (17.888 s) 185 km/h where 184.7 was expected; its Date & Time read `first_gps_timestamp` plus the media time both times (less its usual 30 minutes). So records 100 ms apart in real time land where they belong. The first frame, taken as the stop notice less the video length on the camera's millisecond counter, came 0.600 to 0.618 s after the start notice on five clips (121, 161 to 164; PRO Video: 0.78 to 0.82 s). `first_gps_timestamp` was the camera's clock reading 0.41, 0.25, 0.05, 0.54 and 0.26 s before it, the average of the start and stop notices' figures (they differ by up to 0.06 s), following nothing found: not a whole-second tick of the clock (the stored fractions were .001, .448, .056, .953) and not the 0.12 to 0.23 s from the start command to the start notice. So `CameraClock` takes the average of the first four, 0.3 s, for Slow Motion (capture state 7, or Slow Motion as the camera's mode before the start notice says, as the stamps are fixed about 0.1 s after the start command); a clip can be 0.25 s out, which Studio's slow playback shows as 2 s. Checked on the phone (clip 164): the build stamped the records 264 ms after their moments with the clock 36 ms ahead, and the clock was read 0.24 to 0.27 s before the first frame, so Studio puts at the first frame the moment 0.03 to 0.06 s after it.
  - Studio offers no dashboard for Timelapse and TimeShift clips: clips 119 and 120 have no Dashboard panel (nor Audio) in the media library or on a project's timeline, with the camera's own records and with the rewritten ones, while the Slow Motion clip beside them has one. Media Info calls 119 a Timelapse. So Studio does not use the GPS in these clips however it is timed, nor a GPX or FIT, which are imported in that panel. The records stay true to real time, and the clip says when each frame was taken (below), so another tool could place them.
  - How these clips run against real time. Timelapse: `GET_TIMELAPSE_OPTIONS` (command 17, `08 01`) answered 500 ms for `MOBILE_TIMELAPSE_VIDEO`, as do metadata fields 30 (`timelapse_interval`) and 113. Clip 119 has 255 frames, 8.508 s at 29.97 fps, taken over 122.4 s, 522 ms apart at first and then 480 ms (14.4 times overall, not the nominal 15). TimeShift: clip 120 has 73 frames, 2.436 s, taken over 67.8 s, 0.33 to 0.40 s apart for the first four and then 0.90 to 1.00 s (28 times), the camera choosing the speed as it goes. Each frame's capture time is in trailer record `0x600`, one `u64` a frame in microseconds on `first_frame_timestamp`'s clock, the first equal to it. TimeShift clips also have `0xc00`, 16-byte pairs of a millisecond time and a number (10, then 60 falling to 54 here), which looks like its speed changes. During a Timelapse run the camera sends `0x2026` with field 1 = 4 about every 13.9 s, counting up in field 8.
  - Interval Photo and Starlapse save each photo as an `IMG_` JPEG of its own: 9 photos (122 to 130) over 38 s, one every 4.4 s although the option reads 3000 ms, and 16 (131 to 146) over 49 s, one every 3.05 s, option 3000 ms. EXIF holds a GPS block of zeros. The GPS records go into each photo's own Insta360 trailer (`0x700`). After every photo the camera sends `0x2026` with field 1 = 5 and then 6 (steps 4, `0x200a`, 5, `0x2012` with the photo count, 6), and the run really ends with capture status `0x2010` state 0 after the stop command (steps 7, 5, 6, then 0). Each step notice carries the capture state in field 4 (3 Interval Photo, 19 Starlapse). The app used to take step 5 as the end of any recording, so it sent GPS for 1.6 s of a 38 s run, its GPX held two points, and only the first photo of each run held records (6 in photo 122, 4 in photo 131). It now ignores steps 5 and 6 whose state is 3 or 19 (`Go2BleProtocol.captureStepEndsRecording`). Checked on the phone (Interval Photo, 9 photos 152 to 160 over 37 s, one every 4.9 s): the app sent 372 records throughout, the GPX held 39 points, and every record reached a photo, each photo holding those that arrived since the photo before it: none in 152, then 48, 51, 49, 50, 50, 50, 49 and 25, each 4.7 to 5.0 s of moments 100 ms apart. As records go out 1 to 2 s after their moment, a photo's last record is about that much older than the photo. Studio shows nothing of a photo's GPS: Media Info gives no location and a photo has no Dashboard panel. The first switch to Interval Photo after connecting was answered 200 but left the camera in Photo, with no `0x2053`; a second took. Starlapse was not run with the fix.
- **Mode notification.** Notification `0x2053` is `CAM_SUBMODE_CHANGE` (8275 in the newer proto, `NotifyCameraSubMode`: field 1 the photo sub-mode, field 2 the video sub-mode, 100 meaning none). The camera sends it together with `0x2021`. It read 100, 5 (`VIDEO_SUPER`) until the owner switched the pod to Video, and 100, 0 (`VIDEO_NORMAL`) after; 5 is PRO Video (100, 5 when the owner chose PRO Video on the pod, 2026-10-16). `GET_OPTIONS` for options 40, 41 and 107 gives the same values on request (100, 0 and Video); the camera fills in both sub-modes, the one it is not in as 100. The app uses both: the status request asks for 40 and 41, and the Video and Photo choice follows them.
- **Camera status shown in the app.** Decoded in `Go2BleProtocol`, held in `CameraDetails`, worded in `CameraStatusText`. Tested against messages captured from the GO 3S: recording time left (notification `0x2021`, field 1 in seconds; the newer proto names it `ENC_STORAGE_REMAIN`, with the photos left in field 2, but a GO 3S sends 0 there and gives the photos left in field 1 while in Photo mode: 1106, as option 10 `REMAINING_PICTURES` also read, and 1105 after one photo, so in Photo mode the app shows it as Photos Left), storage state (`0x2006`), the Action Pod's battery, whether it is connected and whether it is switched on (`0x201E`, `ChargeboxConnectedStatus`; a docked pod that is off shows as Switched Off, not as its battery of 0), the recording mode (capture state in `0x2010` and field 4 of the `0x2026` start notice), and the saved clip's name and length (reply to the stop command, so only for clips stopped from the app). Written from the published definitions only, never seen from the camera: battery low (`0x2004`), shutdown with its reason (`0x2005`), storage full (`0x2007`) and temperature (`0x2016`).
- **Recording already running on connect.** On connecting the app sends a capture status request (command `0x0F`). The camera answers it over Bluetooth with its capture state and the time recorded so far in whole seconds. If it is recording, the stream starts that many seconds plus 1.5 s before the reply, so before the first frame (until 2026-10-17 the first frame was taken as half a second less than that and the stream started there): the missed part is sent first, as records without a fix (or with real positions where the phone has fixes remembered from then, up to 10 minutes back), then the live GPS, and a GPX is saved from the same point. Samples are made only as fast as they can be sent (`MAX_GPS_BACKLOG`), so none is dropped, and at 12 records a second the stream catches up in about five times the delay: a clip joined 12 s late is caught up after a minute, and a clip that ends before then is missing GPS at its end. Since the records are stamped against the camera's clock, a clip joined late is placed as well as one whose start was seen, provided the clock was read on that link before its records go out (they wait up to 15 s for it). Before this was added, a ride clip started 12 s before the app reconnected got no GPS at all. The filling-in has not yet been tried on the camera.
- **Command `0x19` must not be sent.** Earlier versions sent it on every connect, and from a Refresh button, as a "battery query". The published definitions name it `CALIBRATE_GYRO`. Its reply was `100, 1` all 16 times in one day, including five times when the camera's own battery updates said 96% or 98%, and the camera answered it with status 500 while recording. The battery level comes from the camera's own updates (`0x2003`), which arrive when it wakes and when a recording starts or stops, and from the status request below. It is forgotten when the camera stops answering or the link ends, like the other camera details; earlier versions kept the last reading, so a camera switched off still showed its battery. Checked on the phone (2026-10-16): switched off at 100%, the camera dropped the link, the app reconnected to it and showed Camera Is Off with no battery.
- **Status request on connect.** The app sends `GET_OPTIONS` (command `0x08`) with a packed list of option types 9, 11, 20, 85, 40 and 41, and the GO 3S answers over Bluetooth with an `Options` message: recording time left in seconds (field 9, the photos left in Photo mode), its `BatteryStatus` (11), `StorageState` (20, with free and total bytes; 66.7 GB free of 128 GB when captured), `ChargeboxConnectedStatus` (85) and the photo and video sub-modes (40, 41). Option 9 read 5652 where notification `0x2021` had last read 5668 two short clips earlier, which confirms what `0x2021` carries. Free and total space are received but not shown.
- **A camera that is switched off still connects.** A powered-off GO 3S goes on advertising `0xBE80` and accepts a connection, with or without the wake beacon, but sends no sync packet and acts on nothing. Writes are held and answered when it is next switched on: a photo command got status 500 (`msg execute err.`) 56 s after it was sent. The app treats a connected camera as off until it hears from it, and again after 7 s with no answer to the keep-alive (`isCameraAwake`); while it is off the shutter is disabled and no capture command is sent.
- **The wake beacon does not wake the GO 3S.** Broadcast non-connectable and then connectable (three times, 10 s each), it left the camera off both times. Insta360's manual says its own app can wake a powered-off GO series camera.
- **Waking a GO camera that is off (from a Bluetooth capture of the Insta360 app, 2026-10-12).** After connecting and subscribing to `0xBE82`, the app writes one message to `0xBE81`: `FF 0C 02`, a 16-bit length, its authorization id as 36 ASCII characters, and the usual checksum. The camera's Bluetooth chip answers `FF 0D 03 01 00 01` plus checksum 0.3 s later, and 2.1 s after that the camera sends its sync packet: it is on. Two wake-ups were captured and were identical. The id is one the Insta360 app registered with the camera; it also sends it in `CHECK_AUTHORIZATION` (command `0x27`, second field 2) on every connection. It is specific to the phone and is not kept in this repository: Open360 reads it from its preferences (`wake_authorization_id`), set on the Wake-Up ID card or with `adb shell am start -n com.open360/.MainActivity --es wake_authorization_id <id>`. Wake Up opens a fresh link and sends the message before anything else (`buildWakeAuthorizationPacket`, `wakeCamera`). Confirmed from Open360: the camera accepted it 0.2 s after it was sent and was answering 2.1 s later, 4.8 s after Wake Up was pressed. The camera checks the id: one with the wrong first eight characters got `FF 0D 03 01 00 00` and no wake-up. The id travels as ASCII, so in a hex dump its `f` characters show as `66`.
- **Wake-up ID and its set-up in the app.** The id is one the app makes for itself (a random UUID) and the camera has to allow once. The app asks the camera whether it has authorized the id with `CHECK_AUTHORIZATION` (`registerWakeUpId`): when the camera starts answering, when the id is changed, and from the set-up. The reply's field 1 is 0 authorized, 1 unauthorized, 2 busy. For an id it does not know the GO 3S answers unauthorized and asks on its own screen whether to connect to a new app; when that is allowed it sends the result notification `0x2011` with field 1 = 0, and from then on it answers authorized and wakes for the id. The app sends nothing else: it waits up to 60 s for the notification. Seen three times on 2026-10-13, the last through the set-up: check sent, unauthorized 0.2 s later, success 5.8 s after that once it had been allowed on the camera (4.6 s and 2.1 s on earlier runs), and after one of them the camera, switched off, woke for the new id (accepted 0.2 s after it was sent, answering 2.3 s after that). A check is therefore not harmless for an id the camera does not know: it makes the camera ask. The other results in the published definitions (1 rejected, 2 timed out, 3 busy) are handled but have never been seen, so what the camera sends when the question is declined or left unanswered is not known, nor how many ids it keeps or whether the Insta360 app's still works after others are allowed. `REQUEST_AUTHORIZATION` (command `0x56`) and `SET_OPTIONS` with option 38 are not needed and are not sent. In the app: the set-up (`WakeUpSetupDialog`, steps worked out in `WakeUpSetup`) is put forward once on the first run without an id, and whenever Wake Up is pressed without one: switch the camera on, allow Open360 on the camera, ready. Checked on the phone: it opened by itself on a first run, and run with the camera already on it went from asking to done. Its first step, waiting for a camera that is off or out of reach, has not been seen on the phone. For someone who put the set-up off, the main screen shows a Set Up Wake Up card (`WakeUpSetupCard`) at the top for as long as the set-up is still needed (`WakeUpSetup.needed`: no id, the camera asking, or a failed try); it can be dismissed, which is remembered until the camera next allows the app. The id itself is kept off the main screen: the settings button in the top bar opens a Settings screen (`SettingsScreen`, switched to from `Open360App`, which also hosts the two dialogs) with Set Up Wake Up, which shows where the set-up stands and opens it, and Wake-Up ID, which opens a dialog (`WakeUpIdDialog`) showing the id and what the camera says about it, with Copy and Change (paste one from another phone, or make a new one). Checked on the phone: the Settings screen, both of its entries and the way back. The card has not been seen on the phone yet, as the phone used was already set up.
- **The Action Pod after a Bluetooth wake-up.** Woken in its pod with both off, the camera comes on and the pod stays off. The camera's `ChargeboxConnectedStatus` then reads connected 1, on/off 0, USB 1, Bluetooth 1; with the pod on it reads 1, 1, 2, 2, and just after the pod is switched off 0, 0, 2, 1, with the pod's battery level given as 0 whenever it is off. Field 3 is `chargebox_onoff_status` in the published definitions (0 offline, 1 online). Earlier versions of the app did not read it and showed a docked pod that was off as 0%; it now shows Switched Off. Checked on the phone (2026-10-16): after a Bluetooth wake-up the camera's status reply held `0801120408001000180020012801` and the app showed Switched Off, and when the pod was switched on the camera sent `0801120408011064180120022802` and the app showed 100%, Plugged In. The Insta360 app sent nothing about the pod over Bluetooth after its own wake-up: only option requests, a capture status request and the authorization check, and then the camera's Wi-Fi came up (`0x2017`, 15 s after waking) and the app asked for the Wi-Fi name and password. After both wake-ups from Open360 that were watched, the camera stopped answering 5 s and 23 s later and the link timed out; what was done to the camera in that time is not recorded. The pod does not come on when the Insta360 app wakes the camera either (the owner's observation), so this looks like how the camera's Bluetooth wake-up works. No command that switches the pod on is known. - **Sports sensors (2026-10-16).** Heart rate, cadence and power come from Bluetooth sensors over the standard profiles: Heart Rate (`0x180D`, measurement `0x2A37`), Cycling Speed and Cadence (`0x1816`, `0x2A5B`, cadence from crank revolutions and their 1/1024 s event times) and Cycling Power (`0x1818`, `0x2A63`, with cadence from its crank data when present). ## 3. Architecture A GATT Layout & Wire Protocol

### GATT Services & Handles

- Primary Service `0xCE80` (`0000ce80-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb`):
  - `0xCE81` (Write / WriteNoResp): Camera writes status packets (`0xFE 0xEF 0xFE ...`).
    - Type `0x07`: Camera serial handshake (e.g. `8JY3HF`).
    - Type `0x10`: Camera display string containing live recording timer (e.g., `.00:00:05`) or idle state (e.g., `4K|30|UW,  13h09m`).
  - `0xCE82` (Notify + CCCD `0x2902`): Phone notifies camera with:
    - 10 Hz GPS stream (Type `0x83`).
    - Button events (Type `0x86`).
  - `0xCE83` (Read): Static remote identifier (`0x01`, `0x02`).
- Secondary Service `0xD0FF` (`0000d0ff-3c17-d293-8e48-14fe2e4da212`):
  - Characteristics `FFD1` through `FFE0` exposing device info and firmware version (`1.0.0`).

### 10 Hz GPS NMEA Sentence Encoding (0xCE82)

- Frame format: `0xFC 0xEF 0xFE 0x83 0x00 <len> ,26.7,\x07,$GNRMC,...*CS`
- Wire quirks:
  - Longitude is signed and the hemisphere indicator is always literal `'E'` (e.g. 74°W → `-7400.0000,E`).
  - Extra field `'V'` sits between mode character and checksum: `...,A,V*CS`.
- Liveness keep-alive: When GPS fix is not yet acquired, the app continuously streams `$GNRMC` sentences with status `'V'` (void). If streaming stops, the camera drops connection.

### Remote Button Commands (0xCE82)

- Frame format: `0xFC 0xEF 0xFE 0x86 <SN> 0x03 0x01 <BTN> <STATE>`
  - `SN`: Increments by 2 on every button event (resets to 0 upon camera connection).
  - `BTN`: `0x02` Shutter, `0x01` Mode cycle, `0x00` Power.
  - `STATE`: `0x00` Click, `0x03` Held.
- Power off quirk: Power off requires streaming held-power frames (`STATE = 0x03`) every 20 ms for ~1.5 seconds with advancing `SN`. A single click only toggles the screen.
- These frames are for the dormant Architecture A server only. Written to `0xBE81` on the GO 3S they do nothing (section 2).

### Camera Wake-Up Beacon (ORBIT iBeacon)

- Sleeping cameras do not advertise. They wake upon detecting an Apple manufacturer advertisement (`0x004C`, iBeacon envelope `0x02 0x15`).
- Payload (24 bytes): `0x02 0x15 0x09 'O' 'R' 'B' 'I' 'T' 0x09 0xFF 0x0F 0x00 <6-byte ASCII Camera Serial Suffix> 0x00 0x00 0x00 0x00 0xE4 0x01`.

## 4. Key Codebase Files & Responsibilities

### Core BLE & Camera Control
- `GpsRemoteBleManager.kt`: The active BLE manager for Architecture B. Handles connection to `0xBE80`, sync packet processing, keep-alive transmission every 2 s, status/capture notifications (`0x2026`, `0x2010`, `0x2003`, etc.), `GET_OPTIONS` queries, reading and setting the camera's clock against GPS time, wake authorization messages, sequential write queue, 53-byte `UploadGps` streaming, reconnecting by itself after a drop soon after the camera starts answering, and letting go of a link the camera has not answered on within 3 s (held, idle and with no keep-alive, while the Wake-Up set-up is open).
- `ReconnectPolicy.kt`: Decides whether a dropped link is opened again by itself: within 60 s of the camera starting to answer, or a reconnection that failed, at most three times in a row.
- `Go2BleProtocol.kt`: Protobuf and BLE packet framer with CRC-16/MODBUS. Encodes `UploadGps` (code 53) payloads, inner messages, sync responses, authorization check packets, the `SET_OPTIONS` sub-mode switch between Video and Photo, and decodes incoming camera notifications (`CameraDetails`, `CameraCaptureState`, `BatteryStatus`, `StorageState`, `SubModes`). Also preserves Architecture A NMEA frame encoders.
- `CameraClock.kt`: The camera's clock against GPS time: its offset from timed option 12 readings, a set planned to land within half a second of GPS time, and how much later than its moment each GPS record is stamped (the offset less the mode's read point).
- `CameraMode.kt`: The GO 3S's ten capture modes by their Action Pod names, with the sub-mode that selects each and the start, stop or take-picture command and payload that captures in it.
- `CameraWakeUpBleManager.kt`: Wake beacon helper (preserves ORBIT beacon builder).
- `InstaGpsRemoteGattServer.kt`: Emulated GATT server (`0xCE80` & `0xD0FF`) for Architecture A (dormant on GO 3S).

### GPS Processing, Pacing & GPX Recording
- `GpsTrackerEngine.kt`: Android LocationManager provider for satellite-only fixes (`GPS_PROVIDER`), converting altitude to sea level on Android 14+.
- `GpsTrackerService.kt`: Location foreground service that lets the app receive GPS fixes and keep the camera link with the screen off, holding a partial wake lock only while the camera is answering. It has no GPS engine of its own; the fixes come from `CameraViewModel`'s.
- `GpsTimeline.kt`: Circular buffer of recent fixes with linear interpolation to compute exact position, speed, and heading at any millisecond timestamp.
- `GpsStreamSchedule.kt`: Manages 100 ms moment scheduling from the start of a recording's GPS stream, a little before its first frame, catching up late recordings with blank or backlog fixes.
- `GpsSendPacer.kt` & `FixedRateLoop.kt`: Rates and batches GPS uploads at <= 12 records/sec and max 4 together to prevent camera buffer dropouts.
- `GpxTrackRecorder.kt` & `GpxWriter.kt`: Records raw fixes and outputs formatted GPX files to `Download/Open360`, renaming to match the video clip name when stopped from the app.
- `FitWriter.kt`: Encodes a track as a FIT activity (file_id, timer events, one record a second with position, altitude, speed and distance, and heart rate, cadence and power when sensors gave them, then lap, session and activity), written by `GpxTrackRecorder` next to each GPX for importing into Insta360 Studio.
- `SportsSensorManager.kt`, `SensorProfiles.kt`, `SensorTrack.kt` (in `core/sensors`): links to the saved Bluetooth heart-rate, cadence and power sensors, decodes their standard measurements, and logs the readings that `GpxTrackRecorder` passes to `FitWriter`.

### UI & Navigation
- `Open360App.kt`: Root composable managing navigation between `CameraScreen` and `SettingsScreen`, hosting `WakeUpSetupDialog` and `WakeUpIdDialog`.
- `CameraScreen.kt`: Primary UI with speed readout, telemetry grid, status badges, the camera card's one link button, and tactile shutter.

## 5. Connection Workflow

1. Wake the camera.
2. Open Open360. It scans for the camera's `0xBE80` advertisement and connects on its own, without switching the camera on; a camera that is off is let go after 3 s. Tap Connect if it is not connected (once Wake Up is set up, Connect also switches the camera on).
3. Wait for a GPS fix, then record from the app or with the camera's own button. The app sends GPS only while a recording is running and saves a GPX for it on the phone.
4. Once the app shows the camera as connected, the display can be turned off. A foreground service and a wake lock run for as long as the camera stays connected (the Background GPS Tracking switch in Settings, on by default, and remembered), so fixes keep arriving, the keep-alive keeps going and recordings started on the camera are still seen. Android only lets the service start while the app is on screen, so connect before locking the phone. If the link drops within a minute of the camera starting to answer, as it can just after the camera is switched on, the app reconnects on its own; any later drop, such as switching the camera off after a session, leaves it disconnected, and so does a reconnection to a camera that was switched off. Tap Connect to switch it on again; a camera switched on with its own button is not noticed until Connect is tapped. Checked on 2026-10-12: with the display off for 57, 14 and 105 s a fix arrived in every second and the keep-alives stayed 2 s apart, and two clips started on the camera with the phone locked got their GPS.

The Bluetooth Remote menu on the camera is not used.
