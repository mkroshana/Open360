# Open360 — Project Overview & Architecture Guide

## 1. Purpose & Scope

Open360 is an open-source Android application (`com.open360`, Jetpack Compose + Kotlin) designed to replace the official Insta360 GPS Action Remote and commercial watch remote apps (`com.kotlin.insta360gpswatchremote`, `com.aktuna.wear.insta360btctrl`) for Insta360 cameras (GO 3, GO 3S, X3, X4, Ace).

Its primary objective is embedding real-time GPS coordinates directly into recorded video metadata (`.insv`/`.mp4`) while providing camera wake-up, shutter, mode switching, and live recording/battery status tracking over BLE without requiring Wi-Fi live preview.

## 2. Core Protocol Architecture

Insta360 cameras have two completely separate BLE architectures. Open360 uses Architecture B on the GO 3S:

| Mode | Role | GATT Service | GPS Injection Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Architecture A (GPS Remote) | Phone = GATT Server (Peripheral)<br>Camera = Central Client | `0xCE80`, `0xD0FF` | What the real remote does: the camera subscribes to `0xCE82` and stores the streamed NMEA. Not usable from a phone on the GO 3S: the camera never opened a link to the phone. The server code is kept but not started. |
| Architecture B (Direct Control) | Phone = Central Client<br>Camera = Peripheral Server | `0xBE80` (`0xBE81`/`0xBE82`) | Works. `UploadGps` (command `0x35`) over `0xBE81` is stored in the clip. This is how the official app does it. |

### Status on the GO 3S (Verified 2026-10-12, Firmware v9.0.59)

- **Architecture B stores GPS.** Three clips recorded while Open360 was connected each contain GPS records (trailer record `0x700`) matching what the app sent. Clips recorded without it contain none.
- **`UploadGps` format.** Protobuf with one field, `bytes gps = 1`, holding 53-byte little-endian records: unix seconds (8), milliseconds (2), status `A`/`V` (1), latitude (8), `N`/`S` (1), longitude (8), `E`/`W` (1), speed m/s (8), track degrees (8), altitude m (8). An earlier attempt that sent three raw doubles as the payload stored nothing.
- **How the camera and Studio treat the stream.** The camera keeps every GPS record it receives from the start command to the stop command, in arrival order (a clip held exactly the 435 records sent in that window). The first video frame comes about 1.8 s after the start command (GPS kept minus video length was 1.75 to 1.81 s on five clips). Insta360 Studio shows the first kept record on the first frame and the rest 100 ms apart by counting: its Date & Time overlay equals `first_gps_timestamp` plus the video time on every clip checked. Arrival times after the first do not matter, but the pace does: the camera drops records that arrive in a burst. After 20 records in 2 s it took 20 more in half a second and then lost the next 33, which shifted the rest of that clip's GPS by 3.3 s.
- **What the app sends.** Nothing while idle. For a recording, record k is the position at `first frame + k * 100 ms`, interpolated between the real fix before and after that moment (`GpsTimeline`, `GpsStreamSchedule`), with that moment as its timestamp. Records become ready each time a new fix arrives and are sent at no more than 12 a second, at most four together (`GpsSendPacer`), so the backlog after a GPS outage does not arrive as a burst. The pacing has not yet been tested on the camera after an outage. A moment with no fix within 2.5 s on both sides is sent void, after waiting at most 4 s. Nothing is predicted, so the moments after the last fix that arrived before the stop, about 1 to 2 s, have no records.
- **Finding the first frame.** Counted from the camera's start notice (notification `0x2026`, field 1 = 2), and it depends on who started the recording. Started from the app, the notice comes about 0.2 s after the start command and the first frame 1.64 s after the notice (1.61 and 1.67 s on two clips). Started with the camera's button, the notice comes well into the start-up and the first frame is taken as 0.55 s after it (0.2 to 0.4 s on four clips, raised by 0.2 s after the ride check below); using the app figure there put the GPS about 1.3 s ahead of the picture. Both figures are the stop minus the video length, so they take the video to end at the stop. That has not been checked against a filmed clock. The recording ends at field 1 = 5 or 6, or capture state 0. A recording already running when the app connects is not seen.
- **Checked against the camera's motion sensors (ride, 2026-10-12).** A clip holds the camera's own motion, timed against the frames by `first_frame_timestamp`: in Video mode record `0x1900`, one orientation per frame (40 bytes: `u64` microseconds, then a quaternion as 4 doubles); in PRO Video mode record `0x300`, raw accelerometer and gyroscope at 1 kHz (20 bytes: `u64` microseconds, then six `u16` with 32768 as zero, accelerometer first). Lining up its turns and speed changes with the GPS as Studio places it showed the GPS 0.55 s behind the picture on a clip started from the app and about 0.8 s behind on one started with the button. Each video moment is now given the GPS from 0.55 s later (`GPS_BEHIND_PICTURE_MS`) and the button figure was raised by 0.2 s. This rests on two clips and is good to about 0.2 s. Measured again after the change on one clip started with the button (PRO Video, 172 s, 1707 records with none lost): the GPS was 0.04 s behind the picture on turns. A clip started from the app has not been measured since the change. Whether the phone's GPS reports late or the video starts later than worked out is not known. The phone's reported speed agrees with the speed worked out from its own positions to within 0.1 s. Neither clip lost a record: 2097 records for 210.2 s and 1066 for 106.9 s.
- **Capture mode.** Recordings started by the app's start command were all saved as Video (`VID_`, capture state 1), and those started with the camera's button as PRO Video (`PRO_VID_`, capture state 17), with the camera left in PRO Video mode throughout. The app's command does not use the mode set on the camera.
- **Camera status shown in the app.** Decoded in `Go2BleProtocol`, held in `CameraDetails`, worded in `CameraStatusText`. Tested against messages captured from the GO 3S: recording time left (notification `0x2021`, field 1 in seconds; the code is not in the published definitions and the meaning is inferred from how the value behaves), storage state (`0x2006`), the Action Pod's battery and whether it is connected (`0x201E`, `ChargeboxConnectedStatus`), the recording mode (capture state in `0x2010` and field 4 of the `0x2026` start notice), and the saved clip's name and length (reply to the stop command, so only for clips stopped from the app). Written from the published definitions only, never seen from the camera: battery low (`0x2004`), shutdown with its reason (`0x2005`), storage full (`0x2007`) and temperature (`0x2016`).
- **Recording already running on connect.** On connecting the app sends a capture status request (command `0x0F`). The camera answers it over Bluetooth with its capture state and the time recorded so far in whole seconds. If it is recording, the first frame is taken as that many seconds plus half a second before the reply, and the stream starts from there: the missed part is sent first, as records without a fix (or with real positions where the phone has fixes remembered from then, up to 10 minutes back), then the live GPS, and a GPX is saved from the same point. Samples are made only as fast as they can be sent (`MAX_GPS_BACKLOG`), so none is dropped, and at 12 records a second the stream catches up in about five times the delay: a clip joined 12 s late is caught up after a minute, and a clip that ends before then is missing GPS at its end. A clip joined late is placed to within about half a second, not the tenth of a second of one whose start was seen. Before this was added, a ride clip started 12 s before the app reconnected got no GPS at all. The filling-in has not yet been tried on the camera.
- **Command `0x19` must not be sent.** Earlier versions sent it on every connect, and from a Refresh button, as a "battery query". The published definitions name it `CALIBRATE_GYRO`. Its reply was `100, 1` all 16 times in one day, including five times when the camera's own battery updates said 96% or 98%, and the camera answered it with status 500 while recording. The battery level comes from the camera's own updates (`0x2003`), which arrive when it wakes and when a recording starts or stops, and from the status request below.
- **Status request on connect.** The app sends `GET_OPTIONS` (command `0x08`) with a packed list of option types 9, 11, 20 and 85, and the GO 3S answers over Bluetooth with an `Options` message: recording time left in seconds (field 9), its `BatteryStatus` (11), `StorageState` (20, with free and total bytes; 66.7 GB free of 128 GB when captured) and `ChargeboxConnectedStatus` (85). Option 9 read 5652 where notification `0x2021` had last read 5668 two short clips earlier, which confirms what `0x2021` carries. Free and total space are received but not shown.
- **A camera that is switched off still connects.** A powered-off GO 3S goes on advertising `0xBE80` and accepts a connection, with or without the wake beacon, but sends no sync packet and acts on nothing. Writes are held and answered when it is next switched on: a photo command got status 500 (`msg execute err.`) 56 s after it was sent. The app treats a connected camera as off until it hears from it, and again after 7 s with no answer to the keep-alive (`isCameraAwake`); while it is off the shutter is disabled and no capture command is sent.
- **The wake beacon does not wake the GO 3S.** Broadcast non-connectable and then connectable (three times, 10 s each), it left the camera off both times. Insta360's manual says its own app can wake a powered-off GO series camera.
- **Waking a GO camera that is off (from a Bluetooth capture of the Insta360 app, 2026-10-12).** After connecting and subscribing to `0xBE82`, the app writes one message to `0xBE81`: `FF 0C 02`, a 16-bit length, its authorization id as 36 ASCII characters, and the usual checksum. The camera's Bluetooth chip answers `FF 0D 03 01 00 01` plus checksum 0.3 s later, and 2.1 s after that the camera sends its sync packet: it is on. Two wake-ups were captured and were identical. The id is one the Insta360 app registered with the camera; it also sends it in `CHECK_AUTHORIZATION` (command `0x27`, second field 2) on every connection. It is specific to the phone and is not kept in this repository: Open360 reads it from its preferences (`wake_authorization_id`), set on the Wake-Up ID card or with `adb shell am start -n com.open360/.MainActivity --es wake_authorization_id <id>`. Wake Up opens a fresh link and sends the message before anything else (`buildWakeAuthorizationPacket`, `wakeCamera`). Confirmed from Open360: the camera accepted it 0.2 s after it was sent and was answering 2.1 s later, 4.8 s after Wake Up was pressed. The camera checks the id: one with the wrong first eight characters got `FF 0D 03 01 00 00` and no wake-up. The id travels as ASCII, so in a hex dump its `f` characters show as `66`.
- **Wake-up ID and its set-up in the app.** The id is one the app makes for itself (a random UUID) and the camera has to allow once. The app asks the camera whether it has authorized the id with `CHECK_AUTHORIZATION` (`registerWakeUpId`): when the camera starts answering, when the id is changed, and from the set-up. The reply's field 1 is 0 authorized, 1 unauthorized, 2 busy. For an id it does not know the GO 3S answers unauthorized and asks on its own screen whether to connect to a new app; when that is allowed it sends the result notification `0x2011` with field 1 = 0, and from then on it answers authorized and wakes for the id. The app sends nothing else: it waits up to 60 s for the notification. Seen three times on 2026-10-13, the last through the set-up: check sent, unauthorized 0.2 s later, success 5.8 s after that once it had been allowed on the camera (4.6 s and 2.1 s on earlier runs), and after one of them the camera, switched off, woke for the new id (accepted 0.2 s after it was sent, answering 2.3 s after that). A check is therefore not harmless for an id the camera does not know: it makes the camera ask. The other results in the published definitions (1 rejected, 2 timed out, 3 busy) are handled but have never been seen, so what the camera sends when the question is declined or left unanswered is not known, nor how many ids it keeps or whether the Insta360 app's still works after others are allowed. `REQUEST_AUTHORIZATION` (command `0x56`) and `SET_OPTIONS` with option 38 are not needed and are not sent. In the app: the set-up (`WakeUpSetupDialog`, steps worked out in `WakeUpSetup`) is put forward once on the first run without an id, and whenever Wake Up is pressed without one: switch the camera on, allow Open360 on the camera, ready. Checked on the phone: it opened by itself on a first run, and run with the camera already on it went from asking to done. Its first step, waiting for a camera that is off or out of reach, has not been seen on the phone. For someone who put the set-up off, the main screen shows a Set Up Wake Up card (`WakeUpSetupCard`) at the top for as long as the set-up is still needed (`WakeUpSetup.needed`: no id, the camera asking, or a failed try); it can be dismissed, which is remembered until the camera next allows the app. The id itself is kept off the main screen: the settings button in the top bar opens a Settings screen (`SettingsScreen`, switched to from `Open360App`, which also hosts the two dialogs) with Set Up Wake Up, which shows where the set-up stands and opens it, and Wake-Up ID, which opens a dialog (`WakeUpIdDialog`) showing the id and what the camera says about it, with Copy and Change (paste one from another phone, or make a new one). Checked on the phone: the Settings screen, both of its entries and the way back. The card has not been seen on the phone yet, as the phone used was already set up.
- **The Action Pod after a Bluetooth wake-up.** Woken in its pod with both off, the camera comes on and the pod stays off. The camera's `ChargeboxConnectedStatus` then reads connected 1, on/off 0, USB 1, Bluetooth 1; with the pod on it reads 1, 1, 2, 2, and just after the pod is switched off 0, 0, 2, 1, with the pod's battery level given as 0 whenever it is off. The Insta360 app sent nothing about the pod over Bluetooth after its own wake-up: only option requests, a capture status request and the authorization check, and then the camera's Wi-Fi came up (`0x2017`, 15 s after waking) and the app asked for the Wi-Fi name and password. After both wake-ups from Open360 that were watched, the camera stopped answering 5 s and 23 s later and the link timed out; what was done to the camera in that time is not recorded. The pod does not come on when the Insta360 app wakes the camera either (the owner's observation), so this looks like how the camera's Bluetooth wake-up works. No command that switches the pod on is known. Untried candidates from the published definitions: `OPEN_OLED` (command `0x31`, no fields) and `SET_STANDBY_MODE` (command `0x38`, field 1 = 2 for wake up).
- **What did not wake it.** The ORBIT beacon, connectable or not. The fixed packet `ff0640080008000000b00000016d90` from the Insta360 app's older `Go2BleWakeupCmd` routine, which the camera's Bluetooth chip only sends straight back. A sync packet from the phone. Writing to or subscribing to the `0xB000` service. Asking for options 38 and 97 returned an empty `Options`.
- **Getting a Bluetooth capture.** Turn on Enable Bluetooth HCI snoop log in Developer options, restart Bluetooth, do the thing, then `adb bugreport`: the capture is `FS/data/misc/bluetooth/logs/btsnoop_hci.log` in the zip, with the session before the last Bluetooth restart in `.log.last`. On this phone the `persist.bluetooth.btsnooplogmode` property reads empty even when the setting is on, so check the settings screen instead. The Insta360 app's code cannot be read (its dex files are stored without headers, index tables or method bodies), only its strings.
- **GATT services on the GO 3S.** `0x1800`, `0x1801`, `0xBE80` (`0xBE81` write, `0xBE82` notify), `0xB000` (`0xB001` write, `0xB002` to `0xB004` notify; the Insta360 app has the strings `b000` to `b004`, purpose unknown), `0x180A` (software revision), and `9e5d1e47-5c13-43a0-8635-82ad38a1386f`, the Bluetooth chip maker's firmware update service.
- **Fix source.** Satellite fixes only (`GPS_PROVIDER`), one a second. Altitude is converted to height above sea level on Android 14 and later.
- **Session.** The camera sends a sync packet right after notifications are enabled on `0xBE82`; if it sends nothing it is asleep. A keep-alive (`07 00 00 00 05 00 00` in the BLE envelope) every 2 s keeps the link up; without it the camera dropped the link at about 90 s.
- **The link drops when the camera is switched off, and sometimes when it is switched on (checked 2026-10-15).** The camera's Bluetooth stops sending without ending the link, and the phone gives up on it after the supervision timeout. Every link had a 48.75 ms interval, no latency and a 5 s timeout, and the drop shows as status 147 (HCI reason 8, connection timeout), with "Failed to initiate GATT write" just before it because the last write is never answered. Switching the camera off did this all three times: its last message is an Action Pod status reading 0, 0, 2, 1 (`0x201E`), and about a second later it advertises as off and not linked while the phone still holds the link. Switched on while linked, it happened once in four tries. At 22:05 the camera answered at 22:05:21 and the link timed out at 22:05:28, so it stopped about 2 s after it started answering; it had been off and linked for about 12 minutes. Two switch-ons with the button after 28 s and 40 s off, and one by Wake Up after 77 s off, held. The drops 5 s and 23 s after the Bluetooth wake-ups below look the same. What sets it off at switch-on is not known; a camera left off for longer was not tried again. It is one Bluetooth chip throughout, not a second radio that takes over once the camera is on: the address (`EB:0B:A2:1A:9D:6F`, random static), the six services and the advert are the same off and on. The camera advertises, connectable, the whole time, linked or not. Its manufacturer data (company `0x4F47`) is `01 00 SS LL LL`, with `SS` 00 off and 02 on, and `LL LL` 01 01 while it counts a phone as linked. Just after a switch-off it accepted a link without counting it until it was switched on.
- **Reconnecting by itself.** A link that drops within 60 s of the camera starting to answer on it is opened again 1 s later, straight to the same device without a scan, and so is such a reconnection that fails before the camera answers, at most three times in a row (`ReconnectPolicy`). A link that held for longer, or connecting from the app, allows three more. Any other drop is left as before: disconnected. While it reconnects the app shows Connecting and keeps the foreground service, which Android would not let it start again from the background. Checked on the phone: the camera, woken with Wake Up and switched off 12 s later, dropped the link; the app was linked again 1.2 s after the timeout, kept the service, and the camera, switched back on by hand, answered on the new link 4.5 s after it was up. Not yet seen: the spontaneous drop at switch-on since this was added, reconnecting with the phone locked, and giving up when the camera is gone.
- **Capture status.** Notification `0x2010` carries `CameraCaptureState` in field 1: `1` when a video starts, `0` when it stops. It arrives about 1 s after the clip really starts.
- **Architecture A.** The phone-side server was verified end to end with a PC as the central, but the GO 3S never connected to it. Android advertises from a private address that changes; the real remote and working clones use a fixed one. That is the suspected cause, not a proven one.
- **Local GPX.** Each recording gets its own GPX file in `Download/Open360` on the phone, holding the real fixes taken between its first frame and its stop. The camera's reply to the stop command carries the video path (field 1, then field 1), so a recording stopped from the app is saved as the video's name with `.gpx`, for example `VID_20261012_163049_00_066.gpx`, with the video name and the start and end times inside. A recording stopped on the camera has no reply to read, so its file keeps the name `Open360_<date>_<time>.gpx`.
- **Footage over Wi-Fi (checked 2026-10-15).** The camera turns its Wi-Fi on by itself about 2 s after it starts answering over Bluetooth (notification `0x2017`). Its network is `GO 3S <serial suffix>.OSC` on channel 149, and its password is not `88888888`: the app asks for the name and password over Bluetooth with `GET_OPTIONS` option 36 (`WIFI_INFO`; in the reply's `Options`, field 36 holds the name in field 1 and the password in field 2), keeps them, and joins with `WifiNetworkSpecifier` for the app alone. Earlier versions guessed `88888888`, every join failed with `AUTH_FAILURE_WRONG_PSWD`, and the network suggestion they left made Android try the camera as the phone's main Wi-Fi; the app now withdraws it. The camera's OSC HTTP API answers `/osc/info` and `/osc/state`, but refuses `camera.listFiles` with `disabledCommand` and the folder `/DCIM/Camera01/` with 403. Files are listed with `GET_FILE_LIST` (command 13: media type 2, start, limit), which the camera answers over Bluetooth and over Wi-Fi port 6666 alike (the same protobuf messages, behind a 4-byte length and a 12-byte header; a reply carries its request's sequence number): 74 paths in 0.08 s, newest first, with no sizes. Each video is a full clip and a low-resolution copy, for example `PRO_VID_20261015_181505_00_095.mp4` (358 MB) and `PRO_LRV_20261015_181505_01_095.lrv` (22 MB); photos are `IMG_..._00_001.jpg` with a thumbnail in their EXIF data. Files are served over HTTP at `http://192.168.42.1/<path>` with byte ranges: 19 MB/s to the PC, and a 126 MB clip reached the phone in under 10 s. `GET_MINI_THUMBNAIL` (command 30) is answered 400, "unknown msg code". In the app each clip is listed once, with the first frame of its copy as the thumbnail and the date from its name; Preview streams the copy and Download saves the full clip to `Download/Open360`. Checked on the phone: joining, the list, thumbnails of clips and photos, a preview, a photo, and downloading and playing a clip. Not checked: joining with the saved password while Bluetooth is off, and a list longer than one page of 500.

## 3. Architecture A GATT Layout & Wire Protocol

### GATT Services & Handles

- Primary Service `0xCE80` (`0000ce80-0000-1000-8000-00805f9b34fb`):
  - `0xCE81` (Write / WriteNoResp): Camera writes status packets (`0xFE 0xEF 0xFE ...`).
    - Type `0x07`: Camera serial handshake (e.g. `8JY3HF`).
    - Type `0x10`: Camera display string containing live recording timer (e.g., `.00:00:05`) or idle state (e.g., `4K|30|UW,  13h09m`).
  - `0xCE82` (Notify + CCCD `0x2902`): Phone notifies camera with:
    - 10 Hz GPS stream (Type `0x83`).
    - Button events (Type `0x86`).
  - `0xCE83` (Read): Static remote identifier (`0x01`, `0x02`).
- Secondary Service `0xD0FF` (`0000d0ff-3c17-d293-8e48-14fe2e4da212`):
  - Characteristics `FFD1` through `FFE0` exposing device info and firmware version (`1.0.0`).

### 10 Hz GPS NMEA Sentence Encoding (0xCE82)

- Frame format: `0xFC 0xEF 0xFE 0x83 0x00 <len> ,26.7,\x07,$GNRMC,...*CS`
- Wire quirks:
  - Longitude is signed and the hemisphere indicator is always literal `'E'` (e.g. 74°W → `-7400.0000,E`).
  - Extra field `'V'` sits between mode character and checksum: `...,A,V*CS`.
- Liveness keep-alive: When GPS fix is not yet acquired, the app continuously streams `$GNRMC` sentences with status `'V'` (void). If streaming stops, the camera drops connection.

### Remote Button Commands (0xCE82)

- Frame format: `0xFC 0xEF 0xFE 0x86 <SN> 0x03 0x01 <BTN> <STATE>`
  - `SN`: Increments by 2 on every button event (resets to 0 upon camera connection).
  - `BTN`: `0x02` Shutter, `0x01` Mode cycle, `0x00` Power.
  - `STATE`: `0x00` Click, `0x03` Held.
- Power off quirk: Power off requires streaming held-power frames (`STATE = 0x03`) every 20 ms for ~1.5 seconds with advancing `SN`. A single click only toggles the screen.

### Camera Wake-Up Beacon (ORBIT iBeacon)

- Sleeping cameras do not advertise. They wake upon detecting an Apple manufacturer advertisement (`0x004C`, iBeacon envelope `0x02 0x15`).
- Payload (24 bytes): `0x02 0x15 0x09 'O' 'R' 'B' 'I' 'T' 0x09 0xFF 0x0F 0x00 <6-byte ASCII Camera Serial Suffix> 0x00 0x00 0x00 0x00 0xE4 0x01`.

## 4. Key Codebase Files & Responsibilities

### Core BLE & Camera Control
- `GpsRemoteBleManager.kt`: The active BLE manager for Architecture B. Handles connection to `0xBE80`, sync packet processing, keep-alive transmission every 2 s, status/capture notifications (`0x2026`, `0x2010`, `0x2003`, etc.), `GET_OPTIONS` queries, wake authorization messages, sequential write queue, 53-byte `UploadGps` streaming, and reconnecting by itself after a drop soon after the camera starts answering.
- `ReconnectPolicy.kt`: Decides whether a dropped link is opened again by itself: within 60 s of the camera starting to answer, or a reconnection that failed, at most three times in a row.
- `Go2BleProtocol.kt`: Protobuf and BLE packet framer with CRC-16/MODBUS. Encodes `UploadGps` (code 53) payloads, inner messages, sync responses, authorization check packets, and decodes incoming camera notifications (`CameraDetails`, `CameraCaptureState`, `BatteryStatus`, `StorageState`). Also preserves Architecture A NMEA frame encoders.
- `CameraWakeUpBleManager.kt`: Wake beacon helper (preserves ORBIT beacon builder).
- `InstaGpsRemoteGattServer.kt`: Emulated GATT server (`0xCE80` & `0xD0FF`) for Architecture A (dormant on GO 3S).

### GPS Processing, Pacing & GPX Recording
- `GpsTrackerEngine.kt`: Android LocationManager provider for satellite-only fixes (`GPS_PROVIDER`), converting altitude to sea level on Android 14+.
- `GpsTrackerService.kt`: Foreground service holding a partial wake lock so fixes, keep-alive, and camera notifications continue with screen off.
- `GpsTimeline.kt`: Circular buffer of recent fixes with linear interpolation to compute exact position, speed, and heading at any millisecond timestamp.
- `GpsStreamSchedule.kt`: Manages 100 ms video moment scheduling from the recording's first frame, catching up late recordings with blank or backlog fixes.
- `GpsSendPacer.kt` & `FixedRateLoop.kt`: Rates and batches GPS uploads at <= 12 records/sec and max 4 together to prevent camera buffer dropouts.
- `GpxTrackRecorder.kt` & `GpxWriter.kt`: Records raw fixes and outputs formatted GPX files to `Download/Open360`, renaming to match the video clip name when stopped from the app.

### UI & Navigation
- `Open360App.kt`: Root composable managing navigation between `CameraScreen` and `SettingsScreen`, hosting `WakeUpSetupDialog` and `WakeUpIdDialog`.
- `CameraScreen.kt`: Primary UI with speed readout, telemetry grid, status badges, and tactile shutter.
- `SettingsScreen.kt`: Settings screen for Background GPS Tracking toggle, Set Up Wake Up status, and Wake-Up ID management.
- `WakeUpSetup.kt`, `WakeUpSetupCard.kt`, `WakeUpSetupDialog.kt`: Step-by-step pairing wizard guiding the camera authorization prompt and tracking setup state.
- `WakeUpIdDialog.kt`: Dialog to inspect, copy, or change the camera's 36-char wake-up authorization ID.
- `CameraStatusText.kt`: Plain-English formatters for storage, battery, runtime, Action Pod, and warning conditions.
- `CameraViewModel.kt`: Central state orchestrator connecting BLE, GPS, settings, and UI flows.

### Media Browser, Wi-Fi Transfer & Player
- `CameraWifiConnector.kt`: Programmatic Wi-Fi connection and process network binder. Joins the camera's access point for the app alone via `WifiNetworkSpecifier`, with the name and password the camera gives over Bluetooth, and binds the app process (`bindProcessToNetwork`) so requests to `192.168.42.1` go to the camera over Wi-Fi STA concurrency while Cellular/home Wi-Fi keeps the internet. Withdraws the network suggestion earlier versions registered with the wrong password.
- `FootageRepository.kt`: Manages media discovery, downloading, and importing across three sources:
  1. Camera Wi-Fi: file list over port 6666 (`GET_FILE_LIST` through `CameraSocketClient`), downloads and streaming over HTTP on port 80, thumbnails from each clip's `.lrv` copy and from photos' EXIF data.
  2. Local storage (`Download/Open360` and `DCIM/Insta360Download`) via `MediaStore.Downloads` and `MediaStore.Video.Media`.
  3. Wired USB-C OTG / Storage Access Framework (SAF) folder tree (`DCIM/Camera01`).
- `CameraFileList.kt`: Turns the camera's listed paths into list entries, attaching each clip's `.lrv` copy as its preview and reading the date from the file name.
- `CameraSocketClient.kt`: Client for the camera's port 6666 (sync, keep-alive, notifications), with `request()` matching each reply to its command by sequence number.
- `FootageViewModel.kt`: State management for camera Wi-Fi, downloaded, and USB footage tabs. When the camera tab opens it gets the Wi-Fi name and password from the Bluetooth link (`GpsRemoteBleManager.requestWifiInfo`, or the saved ones) and starts `CameraWifiConnector`.

- `GpxParser.kt`: Universal XML parser extracting point count, total distance, duration, elevation gain, and max speed from companion `.gpx` files.
- `OscClient.kt`: OSC v2 client supporting `/osc/info`, `/osc/state` and capture commands. Not used for listing: the GO 3S refuses `camera.listFiles`.
- `CameraMediaFile.kt`: Unified data model for camera clips across remote HTTP, local storage, and SAF content URIs, with `previewUrl` for a camera clip's `.lrv` copy.

## 5. Connection Workflow

1. Wake the camera.
2. Open Open360. It scans for the camera's `0xBE80` advertisement and connects on its own; tap Connect if it was opened before the camera woke.
3. Wait for a GPS fix, then record from the app or with the camera's own button. The app sends GPS only while a recording is running and saves a GPX for it on the phone.
4. Once the app shows the camera as connected, the display can be turned off. A foreground service and a wake lock run for as long as the camera stays connected (the Background GPS Tracking switch in Settings, on by default, and remembered), so fixes keep arriving, the keep-alive keeps going and recordings started on the camera are still seen. Android only lets the service start while the app is on screen, so connect before locking the phone. If the link drops within a minute of the camera starting to answer, as it can just after the camera is switched on, the app reconnects on its own; any later drop, such as switching the camera off after a session, leaves it disconnected, so tap Connect before switching the camera on again. Checked on 2026-10-12: with the display off for 57, 14 and 105 s a fix arrived in every second and the keep-alives stayed 2 s apart, and two clips started on the camera with the phone locked got their GPS.

The Bluetooth Remote menu on the camera is not used.
